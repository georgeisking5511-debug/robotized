ADJECTIVES HOMEWORK — ANSWERS, TRANSLATIONS & STUDY GUIDE
(Source: Book pages 7–8, “Adjectives homework” worksheet)
================================================================================

--- WORKSHEET IN ENGLISH (what each exercise asks) ---------------------------

SECTION 1 — “Give the opposite adjectives as shown in the cue sentence.”
  Cue: “Khan Market is an expensive bazaar, but INA bazaar is a cheap bazaar.”
  Cue: “My tea is cold, but your tea is hot.”
  You fill each blank with the opposite adjective (gender/number should match
  the noun in the sentence).

SECTION 2 — “Put the appropriate adjectives.”
  Choose suitable Hindi adjectives for each sentence (more than one answer
  may be acceptable).

SECTION 3 — “Translate these sentences.”
  Translate the English sentences into Hindi.

EXTRA — “Make sentences with these words.”
  Build your own Hindi sentences using: sundar, thandā, mahangā, log, paise,
  mushkil, sabzi, dukān, kapre, pariwār.

================================================================================
SECTION 1 — ANSWERS (opposites; Hindi + English gloss)
================================================================================

(Examples already on sheet: mahangā ↔ sastā; thandī ↔ garm)

1.  India barā deś hai lekin Nepal CHHOTĀ deś hai.
    India is a big country, but Nepal is a SMALL country.
    Answer: chhoṭā

2.  June GARM hai lekin January THANDĀ hai.
    June is HOT, but January is COLD.
    Answer: thandā (mausam implied: “[weather is] cold”)

3.  Ye machine achchhī nahī̃ hai. Ye BURĪ hai.
    This machine is not good. It is BAD.
    Answer: burī (f., agrees with machine)

4.  Āpka homework āsān hai, lekin mera homework MUSHKIL hai.
    Your homework is easy, but my homework is DIFFICULT.
    Answer: mushkil

5.  Mera khānā bahut THANDĀ hai, lekin āpka khānā garm hai.
    My food is very COLD, but your food is hot.
    Answer: thandā

6.  Glass me THODĀ/KAM pānī hai, lekin jug me bahut pānī hai.
    There is LITTLE / A LITTLE water in the glass, but a lot of water in the jug.
    Answer: thodā / kam (thodā is very common in textbooks)

7.  Merī chāy thandī hai lekin āpkī chāy GARM hai.
    (Same pattern as the cue.) Answer: garm / garmī (garm is fine)

8.  Paris ek MAHANGĀ shahār hai, Jaipur sastā shahār hai.
    Paris is an EXPENSIVE city; Jaipur is a cheap city.
    Answer: mahangā

9.  Five star me amīr log haĩ. Slum me GARĪB log haĩ.
    In a five-star [hotel] there are rich people. In the slum there are POOR people.
    Answer: garīb

10. Mera ghar āj sāf nahī̃, GANDĀ hai.
    My house is not clean today; it is DIRTY.
    Answer: gandā

11. Ye qilā bahut purānā hai, The White House NAYĀ hai.
    This fort is very old; the White House is NEW.
    Answer: nayā / navīn (nayā is usual in class)

12. Merī dress kālī hai lekin āpkī kamīz SAFED hai.
    My dress is black, but your shirt is WHITE.
    Answer: safed / sāfēd

13. Monday bāzār KHULĀ hai lekin Sunday ko bāzār BAND hai.
    On Monday the market is open, but on Sunday the market is CLOSED.
    Answer: band

14. Movie me villain BURĀ hai lekin hero achchhā hai.
    In the movie the villain is BAD, but the hero is good.
    Answer: burā

15. Pizza MAHANGĀ hai, samosā sastā hai.
    Pizza is EXPENSIVE; the samosa is cheap.
    Answer: mahangā (or mahangī if you treat “pizza” as f. — both heard)

16. Mera chhoṭā bhāī France me hai lekin mera BARĀ bhāī Brazil me hai.
    My younger brother is in France, but my ELDER / BIG brother is in Brazil.
    Answer: baṛā (here = elder)

17. Dukān me tāzī sabzī nahī̃, PURĀNĪ/BASĪ sabzī hai.
    In the shop the vegetables are not fresh; they are OLD/STALE.
    Answer: purānī (f., agrees with sabzī) — or basī in some regions

SECTION 3 — ENGLISH → HINDI (translations for the given sentences)
================================================================================

 1. This is very cold food.
    Yah bahut thandā khānā hai.   (or: Yah khānā bahut thandā hai.)

 2. She is a poor girl but she is very nice.
    Vo garīb laṛkī hai lekin bahut achchhī hai.

 3. This is my work, not your work.
    Yah merā kām hai, āpkā kām nahī̃.

 4. Is your work easy?
    Kyā āpkā kām āsān hai?

 5. Today the weather is hot.
    Āj mausam garm hai.

 6. My car is red but your car is black.
    Merī gāṛī lāl hai lekin āpkī gāṛī kālī hai.

 7. My room is clean.
    Merā kamrā sāf hai.

 8. This book is very old.
    Yah kitāb bahut purānī hai.

 9. There is little food in the plate.
    Plēṭ me thodā / kam khānā hai.

10. Do you have plenty of money?
    Kyā āp ke pās bahut / kaafī paise haĩ?

11. My house is dirty after the party.
    Party ke bād mera ghar gandā hai.

12. That girl is from my city.
    Vo laṛkī mere shehar se hai.

13. He is a poor man.
    Vo garīb ādmī hai.

14. She is very rich.
    Vo bahut amīr hai.

15. My friend’s house is very small.
    Mere dost kā ghar bahut chhoṭā hai.

16. Are you tired today?
    Kyā āj āp thake hue haĩ?   (or: Āj āp thake hue haĩ?)

17. Delhi’s market is big.
    Dillī kā bāzār baṛā hai.

18. This shirt is blue.
    Yah kamīz / kurta nīlā hai.   (shirt → often kamīz in class Hindi)

19. The market is not open today.
    Āj bāzār nahī̃ khulā / āj bāzār band hai.

20. Delhi is an old city.
    Dillī ek purānā shahār hai.

EXTRA — EXAMPLE SENTENCES (Hindi + English)
================================================================================

 1. Sundar   — Merā ghar bahut sundar hai.     My house is very beautiful.
 2. Thandā   — Āj mausam thandā hai.           Today the weather is cold.
 3. Mahangā  — Yah phal bahut mahangā hai.     This fruit is very expensive.
 4. Log      — Bāzār me bahut log haĩ.         There are many people in the market.
 5. Paise    — Mere pās kam paise haĩ.         I have little money.
 6. Mushkil  — Yah savāl mushkil hai.         This question is difficult.
 7. Sabzi    — Ye sabzī tāzī hai.             This vegetable is fresh.
 8. Dukān    — Merī dukān yahā̃ hai.          My shop is here.
 9. Kapre    — Mere kapṛe sāf haĩ.            My clothes are clean.
10. Pariwār  — Merā pariwār baṛā hai.         My family is big.

FOOTNOTES FROM THE WORKSHEET (translated)
================================================================================

 • “Āp ke pās” = with you / in your possession (used for “Do you have…?”).
 • “Ke bād” = after (e.g. after the party).
